# Main Treatment Effects and Interim Commercial Recommendation

## Decision summary

**Interim recommendation: use the Men's E-Mail treatment as the provisional default campaign among the tested options, subject to confirmation from a direct Men's-versus-Women's comparison and to the organisation's actual margin and contact-cost assumptions.**

Both email treatments generated positive incremental outcomes relative to the no-email control. The Men's E-Mail arm produced the larger observed lift on visits, conversions and spend per eligible customer, and its commercial case remains positive under a wider range of cost assumptions.

This is an intention-to-treat conclusion for the 64,000 customers in the experiment. It is not yet a claim that the Men's treatment is statistically superior to the Women's treatment; that requires a direct head-to-head comparison.

## Observed arm outcomes

| Experiment arm | Customers | Visit rate | Conversion rate | Revenue per customer | Average order value among converters |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No E-Mail | 21,306 | 10.62% | 0.57% | 0.653 | 114.00 |
| Mens E-Mail | 21,307 | 18.28% | 1.25% | 1.423 | 113.53 |
| Womens E-Mail | 21,387 | 15.14% | 0.88% | 1.077 | 121.89 |

The Men's treatment increased customer response primarily by generating more visits and conversions rather than by increasing average order value. The Women's arm had the highest observed average order value among converters, but that descriptive difference is not itself a causal treatment effect.

## Men's E-Mail versus control

| Outcome | Absolute effect | Relative lift | 95% confidence interval | Multiplicity-adjusted p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Visit rate | +7.66 percentage points | +72.1% | +7.00 to +8.32 points | < 0.001 |
| Conversion rate | +0.68 percentage points | +118.8% | +0.50 to +0.86 points | < 0.001 |
| Spend per eligible customer | +0.770 | +117.9% | +0.484 to +1.052 | < 0.001 |

The estimated number needed to treat is approximately 13 customers for one additional visit and 147 customers for one additional conversion.

## Women's E-Mail versus control

| Outcome | Absolute effect | Relative lift | 95% confidence interval | Multiplicity-adjusted p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Visit rate | +4.52 percentage points | +42.6% | +3.89 to +5.16 points | < 0.001 |

## Commercial sensitivity per 1,000 eligible customers

The commercial model converts incremental revenue into contribution after contact cost. It excludes fixed campaign costs and any unobserved fulfilment or incentive differences.

### Break-even contact cost per customer

| Treatment | Effect basis | 25% margin | 40% margin | 60% margin |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mens E-Mail | Point estimate | 0.192 | 0.308 | 0.462 |
| Mens E-Mail | Lower 95% confidence bound | 0.121 | 0.194 | 0.290 |
| Womens E-Mail | Point estimate | 0.106 | 0.170 | 0.255 |
| Womens E-Mail | Lower 95% confidence bound | 0.038 | 0.061 | 0.091 |

At a 40% contribution margin and a contact cost of 0.10 per customer, the point-estimate profit per 1,000 eligible customers is approximately:

- **Mens E-Mail: 208**
- **Womens E-Mail: 70**

Using the lower 95% revenue-effect bound under the same assumptions, the estimated profit remains positive for both treatments:

- **Mens E-Mail: 94 per 1,000**
- **Womens E-Mail: 11 per 1,000**

The Women's treatment is therefore more sensitive to contact cost and margin uncertainty.

## Interpretation

1. Both treatments causally increased the pre-specified primary outcome of spend per eligible customer relative to control.
2. The Men's treatment has the stronger observed commercial profile and the wider margin for implementation cost.
3. The campaign decision should be based on incremental contribution, not total revenue observed in the treated groups.
4. The current analysis does not establish which customer segments should receive each treatment.
5. A direct treatment-to-treatment comparison is required before stating that the Men's creative is statistically superior to the Women's creative.

## Provisional action

- Use the Men's treatment as the default benchmark for the next decision stage.
- Retain the Women's treatment as a viable alternative where customer affinity or future segment evidence supports it.
- Proceed to direct treatment comparison and pre-specified segment-effect analysis.
- Do not launch model-based targeting until segment findings are validated on held-out data.
